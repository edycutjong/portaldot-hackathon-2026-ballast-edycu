## How to read it

- **One machine.** In the first and second rows the drain, the watcher and the V3 client all run on one laptop; the V3 node is remote. The third row crosses two clocks (this machine's drain, the Actions runner's inclusion record) and includes the workflow queue, runner start and `pip install`: it is **not** the latency of a scheduled run, and GitHub's cron is not real-time.
- **Local anvil is not Sepolia.** The first row's reserve chain is a local anvil with instant mining; the freeze leg (read vault → decide → sign `M_watch` → V3 inclusion) is the same code, but the provider read is faster than a public Sepolia RPC. The thaw leg in that row uses an emulated finality (`anvil_mine`) and says nothing about Sepolia's measured 14–19 min (841–1,168 s) finality waits.
- **What dominates.** Waiting for the watcher's next poll (the detect column, every 2 V3 blocks of ~6 s) and for inclusion in a ~6 s block; both are mostly waiting, not computation (a provider read takes about 0.01 s on the anvil, median 0.008 s, and typically 0.4–0.6 s on Sepolia, median 0.49 s, in the watcher logs). A sample can land in under one poll interval when the drain happens just before a check.
- **Small n.** With fewer than 20 samples a nearest-rank p95 is simply the largest sample; read the Sepolia rows (n = 6 and 3) as a handful of observed drills, not as a percentile.
- **Fee.** The freeze is `Utility.batch_all[System.remark_with_event, Multisig.as_multi_threshold_1(Assets.freeze_asset)]`; the figure is the on-chain `TransactionPayment.TransactionFeePaid` of that extrinsic.
- **Samples are not independent trials of a service.** They are sequential drills on one asset; the n is a count of drills, not a claim about the long-run distribution.
